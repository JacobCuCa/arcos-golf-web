IThe Project

One of Europe’s very best

Based on the experience of Felipe Ortiz-Patiño, former Vice President and President of Valderrama Golf Club, one of the most prestigious golf courses in Continental Europe and host to numerous major international golf championships, such as the 1997 Ryder Cup or the 1999 and 2000 World Golf Championships, as well as numerous editions of the Volvo Masters, which closed the European Tour season, the objective of Patiño Resorts Arcos and Valderrama Investments SA is to revive and develop a resort called Arcos Gardens, located in the municipality of Arcos de la Frontera in the province of Cádiz, and make it one of Europe’s very best.

Arcos Gardens, a resort and golf course designed by the American company Landmark, was chosen because of the quality and the design of the golf course, which allows it, with adjustments and upgrades, to become one of Europe’s very best and act as the driving force in the recovery and success of the resort. Although it was one of Spain’s best courses between 2010 and 2015, the Arcos Gardens course was never a “must play” course, and so it never became a tourist attraction in the way Sotogrande or Valderrama did.

IThe Project · Objectives

A “must play” course

01

Based on this assessment, the first objective is not only to restore but to noticeably upgrade the existing golf course so it can become a true source of revenue and a top-tier tourist product.

02

The second objective is to equal the quality benchmark set by Valderrama in terms of service and maintenance, and to create one of Europe’s most coveted and prestigious clubs and destinations.

We are convinced that, with an appropriate renovation and redesign programme combined with a substantial increase in the quality of its maintenance, the golf course can not only recover its former position among the Top 100 golf courses in Continental Europe (No. 88 in 2015), but position itself among the very best in Spain and the top 30 in Continental Europe, and become a “must play” course and a venue for major professional events, like nearby Valderrama. With the prestige and expertise of the new ownership and that of Kyle Phillips, the world-renowned architect who has been selected to redesign and upgrade the course, this objective is more than achievable.

The management of the project will be in the hands of Mr Felipe Ortiz-Patiño who, for more than 20 years, together with his father Mr Jaime Ortiz-Patiño, was one of the main driving forces behind the creation of Valderrama, leading this course to be ranked number one in Continental Europe and to host the world’s most prestigious golf events since 1988. Valderrama Investments SA, a company owned by Mr Felipe Ortiz-Patiño, will be the official consultant, so that in fact this project will directly and indirectly combine the names Patiño and Valderrama.

IThe Project · The resort

The cortijo

Early 20th century, on 19th-century foundations

Completing the resort’s inventory is an early 20th-century Andalusian cortijo with foundations dating back to the 19th century. The building, which was operated as a small hotel, comprises ten rooms, a restaurant, lounges, a patio, two terraces, a swimming pool and sports facilities. The hotel offering was increased by the use of existing homes and villas, allowing for an additional 5–10 rooms beyond those of the cortijo itself. Moreover, with its large gardens, the cortijo offers excellent conditions for hosting social events.

Expanding the offer

As a complement to all the activities that can be carried out on site, we plan to expand the offer of the resort with:

  • iHorseback riding
  • iiShooting
  • iiiHiking
  • ivSpa and wellness, in a newly built facility
  • vLake and seaside activities, on the shore of Lake Arcos and later at one of the nearby beaches of Sanlúcar, Rota, Chipiona or El Puerto de Santa María

The goal is to have a global and complete offer, making Patiño Resorts Arcos a modern destination for a large variety of users.

IThe Project · Residential & hotel

The whole resort

The vision of recovering and repositioning the resort is not limited to golf and hospitality: it also embraces the entire residential development. The revival of tourism and sport is what will bring its real estate back to life.

Around 80% of the roads and infrastructure are already built, and the residential plan allows for 64,259 square metres of homes, in up to 412 units.

The infrastructure is of the highest quality and provides all services, and the homes already built are fully in keeping with the repositioning and rebranding of the resort.

The resort also includes a 28,847 square-metre hotel plot, with building rights for 6,700 square metres of floor space (potentially 7,700). This opens the way to a brand-new five-star hotel and, if so decided, an extension to the cortijo with a wellbeing, relaxation and spa unit.

IIProject environment

The location

In addition to the quality and potential of the golf course and that of the entire resort, great consideration has been given to Arcos de la Frontera and its location within the province of Cádiz. The province has been classified by The New York Times as one of the 52 most attractive destinations in the world.

Arcos, a town of 31,267 inhabitants (in 2025), was first a Roman post, “Colonia Arcensis”, later a Moorish fortified town, “Medina Ar-Kosch”, and finally, from 1250 onwards, a Spanish town. It has various historical monuments and is considered one of Spain’s most beautiful and picturesque towns.

Arcos is surrounded by numerous tourist attractions, such as
  1. 01Within 30 minutes, the Sierra de Cádiz, with the route of the “Pueblos Blancos”, which features numerous tourist and natural resources.
  2. 02Arcos Lake, a reservoir spanning over 100 hectares (250 acres), which offers water skiing, wakeboarding, kayaking and other water sports.
  3. 03Jerez de la Frontera, a city internationally known for, amongst other things, its horses, its wineries and its outstanding gastronomic offer.
  4. 04Sanlúcar, Rota, Chipiona and El Puerto de Santa María, and their magnificent clear sand beaches along the Atlantic Ocean shore.
  5. 05Cádiz, one of Spain’s most beautiful cities, founded by the Phoenicians and provincial capital, which offers a first-class cruise port and is also a fashionable destination.
  6. 06Seville, one of the most historically rich cities in Europe which, in addition to its parks and numerous historical monuments, offers fine restaurants and shopping.
  7. 07The Jerez Speed Circuit, only 25 kilometres away. It hosted 7 Formula 1 Grands Prix from 1986 until 1997, as well as the Spanish MotoGP from 1987 to the present day. Many major car manufacturers and motor racing teams, including Formula 1, do pre-season testing there. A racing academy will be created so that residents and clients can enjoy a safe track experience.
  8. 08The San Roque–Alcaidesa region (one of Europe’s best golfing destinations), with its five-star hotels and its mythical courses (Valderrama and Sotogrande), is only 115 kilometres away.
  9. 09Aponiente (3 Michelin stars) and Lú (2 Michelin stars), two of Spain’s best restaurants, are 47 and 32 kilometres away respectively.
  10. 10Marbella: via the Jerez–Los Barrios motorway (A-381) or via Ronda, it is possible to reach Arcos in less than two hours.
  11. 11Major natural parks, namely Los Alcornocales (less than 30 km), the Sierra de Grazalema (38 km) and the UNESCO natural park of Doñana (175 km to the main entrance, but 35 km to the southern ferry entrance).
  12. 12Many wineries, all within a radius of 50 kilometres, including Bodegas Tesalia and Campestral, both less than 5 kilometres from the resort.

Arcos de la Frontera

Your Andalusian home

Arcos de la Frontera rises dramatically above the River Guadalete.

Its white houses, narrow streets, churches, palaces and viewpoints form one of the most recognisable landscapes of inland Cádiz.

The historic centre preserves the urban character inherited from its Arab past and contains landmarks such as the Plaza del Cabildo, the Ducal Castle and the Basilica of Santa María.

But Arcos is not simply beautiful. Its geographical position is what makes it exceptional. It sits between three worlds.

Walking the upper town at dusk, when the stone of Santa María turns golden and the streets empty, is one of the simplest pleasures of a stay here, and it is only minutes from the resort.

Founded by the Phoenicians as Gadir some three thousand years ago, Cádiz is often described as the oldest continuously inhabited city in Western Europe. Its old town sits almost entirely surrounded by water, joined to the mainland by a narrow strip of land.

The Central Market is the place to begin the morning; the Torre Tavira, one of the old merchants’ watchtowers, offers a view over the rooftops. La Caleta, the small beach between the castles of Santa Catalina and San Sebastián, is where the city gathers to watch the sun go down.

The almadraba is a centuries-old way of fishing bluefin tuna with fixed nets as the fish pass along the coast each spring. The village takes its name from this tradition, and the remains of its old almadraba castle still stand beside the beach.

Late spring, when the tuna season is under way, is the moment when Zahara’s kitchens are at their best.

Doñana was declared a National Park in 1969 and a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994. Its marshes, moving dunes and stone-pine forests shelter some of Europe’s rarest species, among them the Iberian lynx and the Spanish imperial eagle, and hundreds of thousands of migrating birds each year.

19Jerez de la Frontera

Three identities

This allows Arcos Golf by Patiño Resorts to offer one of its strongest premium excursions.

Sherry

Centuries-old bodegas and some of the world’s most distinctive wines. Fino, amontillado, oloroso: each ages in oak casks through the solera system, and each owes something to the white albariza soils of the vineyards around the city.

Horses

The tradition of the Andalusian horse. In Jerez it has a home of its own: the Royal Andalusian School of Equestrian Art, where horses and riders perform a choreography of classical dressage.

Flamenco

One of the historic centres of flamenco culture. The city has given the art some of its greatest voices, and the bulería of Jerez is recognised as a style of its own.

Los Alcornocales holds one of the largest cork oak forests in Europe. In its narrow, humid valleys, known locally as canutos, survive fragments of an ancient subtropical woodland of laurels, ferns and rhododendrons.

Cork is still harvested here by hand, stripping the bark from each tree only every nine years or so without harming it.
