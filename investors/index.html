The pedigree · Patiño and Valderrama

The people behind one of Europe’s very best

For more than 20 years, Mr Felipe Ortiz-Patiño and his father, Mr Jaime Ortiz-Patiño, were among the main driving forces behind the creation of Valderrama, leading it to be ranked number one in Continental Europe and to host the world’s most prestigious golf events since 1988.

Management

Official consultant

Valderrama Investments SA, a company owned by Mr Felipe Ortiz-Patiño.

This project will directly and indirectly combine the names Patiño and Valderrama.

The course
The core asset · The course

A Landmark course with championship credentials

Arcos Gardens, a resort and golf course designed by the American company Landmark, was chosen because of the quality and the design of its course, which allows it, with adjustments and upgrades, to become one of Europe’s very best and act as the driving force in the recovery and success of the resort.

Championship

The only championship course in the Sierra de Cádiz, with wide, rolling fairways that follow the natural relief of the land and views over the lake.

Practice

Driving range, pitching and bunker area, two putting greens and three chipping greens. Buggies and clubhouse on site.

Yet it was never a “must play” course, and never became a tourist attraction in the way Sotogrande or Valderrama did. That is the gap the project closes.

The location · Province of Cádiz

Between three worlds

The Atlantic coast, the Cádiz countryside and the mountains. The New York Times has classified the province as one of the 52 most attractive destinations in the world.

Arcos, a town of 31,267 inhabitants (in 2025), was first a Roman post, “Colonia Arcensis”, later a Moorish fortified town, “Medina Ar-Kosch”, and finally, from 1250 onwards, a Spanish town. It is considered one of Spain’s most beautiful and picturesque towns.

Transport and access

An extremely well-connected town

Arcos can be accessed by motorway from Seville (AP-4), Jerez (A-382), Cádiz and the Atlantic coast (AP-4), and the Costa del Sol, Marbella and Sotogrande (A-381). From the Sierra, access is available from Ronda and from Campillos (Málaga).

Jerez has a major railway station, with numerous connections to Madrid. The Alvia high-speed trains put the capital 3 hours 50 minutes from Jerez.

Four international airports, all within a radius of 220 kilometres:

A · Jerez de la Frontera27 min

By motorway. Direct flights to Madrid, Barcelona and other Spanish cities, and some to European countries, especially in summer.
